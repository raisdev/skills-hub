## 1. Photography and video

- **MED-01 [Official]** Use professional photography and video whenever possible. Use high-resolution, properly formatted assets for the intended channel. Avoid enlarging or repurposing low-quality files.
- **MED-02 [Official]** Choose multimedia that is authentic, inclusive, and supports the story being told.
- **MED-03 [Official]** **Cornell Digital Assets** (https://digitalassets.cornell.edu/) is the resource for approved photography. It can be searched and downloaded at sizes suited to Cornell sites and publications.
- **MED-04 [Official]** Unless noted otherwise, photography produced by University Relations is licensed under **Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives 4.0 International** (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/): attribution required (credit, license link, note if changed); noncommercial only; if you remix or transform it you may not distribute the modified material; no added legal or technical restrictions.
- **MED-05 [Official]** Permission to use Cornell assets **not** produced by University Relations comes directly from the college or unit responsible.
- **MED-06 [Official]** Members of the Cornell community may use Cornell-created photography and video resources at https://brand.cornell.edu/design-center/photography/.
- **MED-07 [Derived]** For code: an image must come with a recorded source and license. Do not hot-link brand.cornell.edu gallery images or copy them into a repository without checking MED-04 (CC BY-NC-ND forbids distributing modified versions, so cropping or overlaying text on a University Relations photo and republishing it is not clearly allowed; ask photo@cornell.edu).

## 2. Photo credit format

- **MED-10 [Official]** Credit a photo as `First Last/Cornell University` (the site writes it as "[First, Last Name]/Cornell University").
- **MED-11 [Official]** If the photographer's name is unavailable, credit `Cornell University`.

## 3. AI-generated and AI-edited assets

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/design-center/photography/ ("Artificial Intelligence Use").

- **MED-20 [Official]** AI tools may be used to create or modify design, photo, video and audio assets **only** when the use does not misrepresent reality or compromise the integrity of assets from the Cornell collection.
- **MED-21 [Official]** AI must not be used to fabricate imagery, audio or video that could reasonably be taken as authentic or original documentary content. Creating or altering real or fictitious people, objects or events in a way that misrepresents reality is prohibited.
- **MED-22 [Official]** AI-generated assets (photos, video, audio, and design elements such as graphics or animations) must be **clearly labeled as created using generative AI**.
- **MED-23 [Official]** AI-based enhancement or editing (color correction, cleanup, minor adjustments) is permitted if it does not change the original intent, meaning or editorial context or add new content or significance.
- **MED-24 [Official]** The user is responsible for appropriate use, clear disclosure and compliance.
- **MED-25 [Derived]** When Claude generates an illustration, image or placeholder art for a Cornell-branded page, label it as AI-generated (MED-22) and never generate people, campus scenes or anything that could pass for a Cornell photograph (MED-21). Never generate a Cornell logo or seal (`logos.md`, LOG-01). A chart drawn by code from the user's own data is not generative-AI imagery and needs no AI label; the site does not address that case (Unspecified).

## 4. Music and audio

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/design-center/music.

- **MED-30 [Official]** Cornell provides the **Universal Production Music** database free to current faculty, staff and students for Cornell promotional productions (sign-in via https://www.universalproductionmusic.com/en-us/sso/cornelledu/).
- **MED-31 [Official]** Acceptable: videos showcasing student groups, podcasts or audio that promote the university, videos promoting Cornell or its units, videos for class assignments. Not acceptable: personal use, or anything unrelated to Cornell. Questions: licensing@cornell.edu.

## 5. Copyright and licensing

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/design-center/copyright-licensing.

- **MED-40 [Official]** Unlicensed music, audio, video, photography, images and graphics on university-owned platforms create liability. Anyone creating content as a member of the Cornell community must collect permissions and identify the sources of music and image files.
- **MED-41 [Official]** Each college, school, department or unit must ensure its people do not post digital media to official university sites or affiliated accounts without proper licenses or permission.
- **MED-42 [Official]** Fair use protects reasonable uses in teaching, scholarship and research. Outside those, it has limited application; it generally does not cover videos or multimedia a college, department or student makes for informational or promotional purposes. Those usually need permission and often two separate licenses.
- **MED-43 [Official]** Cornell's campus-wide licenses allow copyrighted music to be *performed* on campus. They do **not** cover putting music in a video or posting music or performances on social media or websites.
- **MED-44 [Official]** Posting recorded music to a website or social channel needs a **mechanical license** from the rights owner. Combining music with images (for example in a video) needs a **synchronization license** from the publisher, and if you use an existing recording, a **master use license** from the artist or label as well.
- **MED-45 [Official]** Images, video and graphics may be used only with the owners' permission. Do not assume anything posted to the internet is free to use.
- **MED-46 [Official]** The site recommends using only public-domain material, properly licensed purchased material, or material created by Cornell. Licenses can take many weeks and may be refused. Stock vendors named: iStockphoto, Adobe Stock, Shutterstock. More help: copyright@cornell.edu and https://copyright.cornell.edu.
- **MED-47 [Official]** Web Communications Standards: all images, graphics, fonts and content must be "licensed or otherwise properly authorized for use" (`typography-web.md`, WEB-22). This covers the Freight and Palatino fonts (TYP-05).

## 6. Releases and consent

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/resources/releases. These are "recommendations and best practices", not hard rules, except where the site says MUST or "always".

- **MED-50 [Official]** Secure signed photo/video release forms whenever possible from identifiable people in marketing or promotional photos and video; archive the digitized release with the asset. Removal requests are **always** honored, even with a signed release.
- **MED-51 [Official]** **Minors:** a parent or guardian's signature is always required if the image will promote any Cornell-related program or activity, in public or private spaces. Any use of releases for minors MUST first be approved by the Office of Risk Management and Insurance (RMI); coordinate with the Youth Safety Team (https://youthsafety.cornell.edu/).
- **MED-52 [Official]** Events: post clear signage at entry points, give advance notice in registration pages or emails, offer an opt-out (one suggested method is a small green sticker), and have photographers avoid people wearing it. Suggested signage language is on the releases page.
- **MED-53 [Official]** Forms: two New York State forms (Cornell students; general public), linked from the releases page. Do not host copies; link to the central pages. Use outside New York State requires the Office of General Counsel (counsel@cornell.edu). Privacy questions: photo@cornell.edu.
