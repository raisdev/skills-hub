## 1. How to use names

- **NOM-01 [Official]** A **formal** written name is for business and legal documents, titles, headers and first impressions. A **standard** written name is slightly shorter, for the first mention in copy and the default in most cases. A **shorthand** written name is the minimum standard for headlines, second and later mentions, and less formal uses.
- **NOM-02 [Official]** Spoken identities have two levels: **standard** (first mention, default) and **shorthand** (second and later mentions, less formal). The written and spoken lists differ in places (for example, "Bowers" alone is not approved in writing but is approved spoken). The entries below give both.
- **NOM-03 [Official]** "**Statutory colleges**" is not an approved term. Cornell's four **contract colleges** are operated under contract with New York state.
- **NOM-04 [Official]** The division of University Relations communicates within AP Style guidelines (https://brand.cornell.edu/messaging). The site does not list AP rules beyond that.
- **NOM-04b [Derived]** The site's own copy writes "New York state" with a lowercase "state", which matches AP Style. Follow that when writing Cornell copy, but it is an observation, not a published rule.

## 2. The ban on "CU"

- **NOM-05 [Official]** "**CU**" is not approved shorthand for Cornell University in any setting. It must not appear in current or future promotion, branding, merchandise, or other communications. Reasons given: it has no affiliation with Cornell, it is commonly associated with the University of Colorado, Columbia and others, it hurts search optimization, acronyms can be opaque to international audiences, and it can be misheard as "see you". Anyone who finds "CU" in use should report the context to Brand Communications. (https://brand.cornell.edu/messaging/abbreviations)
- **NOM-06 [Derived]** When reviewing code, search user-facing strings, page titles, meta tags, alt text, file names shown to users, and generated documents for "CU" as a standalone token. Identifiers that users never see (variable names, CSS classes) are not communications; flag them only as advisory. The site's own release-form filenames begin "CU Release Form", which shows legacy uses exist; the ban still applies to anything you write. A quoted mention that explains the ban (for example in documentation about this rule) is not use as shorthand, but avoid the token in text that publishes automatically, such as a page title, meta description or catalog card.
- **NOM-07 [Official]** The terminology page notes acronyms are "discouraged from use by Cornell brand standards" in general (https://brand.cornell.edu/resources/terminology). Spell a name out on first use.

## 4. Campus and location names

The site's rule: names "prioritize the institution name over the city or location name" and show clear association with the university.

- **NOM-40 [Official] Cornell University.** ✔ Using the university name when including all units at all locations. ✘ Using the university name to speak for a single unit or location.
- **NOM-41 [Official] Ithaca.** ✔ The Cornell University Main Campus; ✔ The Cornell University Main Campus in Ithaca; ✔ The Ithaca Campus. ✘ Cornell Ithaca.
- **NOM-42 [Official] Weill Cornell Medicine campus.** ✔ The Weill Cornell Medicine Campus; ✔ The Weill Cornell Medicine Campus in New York City. ✘ Cornell University NYC Campus; ✘ Cornell NYC Campus; ✘ Weill NYC Campus. Weill Cornell Medicine is a unit of Cornell with locations separate from the main campus.
- **NOM-43 [Official] Qatar.** ✔ The Weill Cornell Medicine Campus in Qatar. ✘ Cornell Qatar Campus; ✘ Cornell Qatar.
- **NOM-44 [Official] Cornell Tech campus.** ✔ The Cornell Tech Campus; ✔ The Cornell Tech Campus in New York City. ✘ Cornell University NYC Campus; ✘ Cornell NYC Campus; ✘ Cornell NYC Tech Campus. Cornell Tech is a unit with locations separate from the main campus.
- **NOM-45 [Official] AgriTech.** ✔ The Cornell AgriTech Campus. ✘ Cornell Geneva. AgriTech in Geneva, New York, is part of CALS on the Ithaca campus. It keeps the designation "New York State Agricultural Experiment Station" (use optional). The "T" in AgriTech is capitalized in all instances.

## 5. Names outside this table

- **NOM-50 [Unspecified]** The nomenclature page covers colleges, schools, the Library, Graduate School, Cooperative Extension, Continuing Education, Weill units, and campuses. It gives no entry for research offices, administrative units, or programs (for example "Research & Innovation" or "RAIS"). Do not call such a name wrong or right. Say the page does not cover it and point to the consultation form (https://apps.univcomm.cornell.edu/brand-forms/forms/consult.html) or brand@cornell.edu.
- **NOM-51 [Official]** The terminology page defines a *naming convention* as a fixed written and verbal identity for services, offices, programs, centers and units, and *nomenclature* as the system of names used in it. It does not supply names for units outside the table.
- **NOM-52 [Derived]** Expanding an acronym on first use (NOM-07) is the safe default for unlisted units. For "RAIS", spell out the unit name on first use if the user can supply it.

## 6. Student organizations and alumni clubs

Sources: https://brand.cornell.edu/messaging/student-orgs and /messaging/alumni.

- **NOM-60 [Official]** Student organization names may use "Cornell" only if the group is formally registered. Standard form: **group name followed by "at Cornell"** (for example "Campus Club at Cornell"). To put "Cornell" in another position, the name must include "Club", "Class" or "Student" (for example "Cornell Chess Club", "Cornell Math Student Club"). Only official teams in the Department of Athletics may use the word "team": "Cornell Dance Team" is not permitted and should be "Dance Club at Cornell". Names are approved at the sole discretion of University Relations via the Use of Name or Logo form.
- **NOM-61 [Official]** Student organizations that are official chapters of outside non-profits must use the "at Cornell" form (for example "Special Olympics Club at Cornell"), cannot be tied to political campaigns or committees, and need approval from University Relations and the outside organization.
- **NOM-62 [Official]** **Required disclaimer**, in the footer of every online property of a student organization: "This organization is a registered student organization of Cornell University."
- **NOM-63 [Official]** Student organizations host sites in the cornell.edu domain only through Campus Groups or the CU Blog Service (the site's own wording includes "CU"; see NOM-06). Three-part domain names (xxx.cornell.edu) are not available to student organizations.
- **NOM-64 [Official]** Student groups may not use Cornell trademarks in their identity or alter them, and must follow the brand guidelines for all websites and artwork and Policy 4.10.
- **NOM-65 [Official]** Alumni clubs and associations may use "Cornell" if formally recognized, with a name that includes "Club", "Association" or "Alumni" (for example "Cornell Club of New Jersey", "Cornell Alumni Association of California"), approved by Alumni Affairs and Development and University Relations. They may not use Cornell trademarks in their own identity, and merchandise requires prior approval.

## 7. Founding principle

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/messaging/founding-principle. Spoken by Ezra Cornell on October 7, 1868, at the dedication of Cornell University and the inauguration of its first president, Andrew Dickson White.

- **NOM-70 [Official]** Complete statement, verbatim:
  `I would found an institution where any person can find instruction in any study.`
- **NOM-71 [Official]** Abbreviated form, "punctuated exactly as shown":
  `... any person ... any study.`
- **NOM-72 [Official]** "Modifying or repurposing the founding principle is expressly prohibited." Questions go to Brand Communications.
- **NOM-73 [Derived]** Reading NOM-70 to NOM-72 together: use only the two approved forms, exactly, and do not paraphrase, reorder, change the punctuation or shorten it any other way. A modified version (for example "Any person, any study") breaks NOM-71 and NOM-72. Whether even an approved form may serve as a tagline or slogan is not stated by the site, and "repurposing" may cover it; treat a tagline use as a Question for Brand Communications.

## 8. Approved boilerplate

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/messaging. The site says these texts "can be used verbatim or adapted".

- **NOM-80 [Official] Brand position.** Cornell is a community of scholars engaged in deep and broad research, teaching tomorrow’s thought leaders to think otherwise and creating and disseminating knowledge with a public purpose.
- **NOM-81 [Official] Mission.** Cornell is a private, Ivy League university and the land-grant university for New York state. Cornell’s mission is to discover, preserve and disseminate knowledge, to educate the next generation of global citizens, and to promote a culture of broad inquiry throughout and beyond the Cornell community. Cornell also aims, through public service, to enhance the lives and livelihoods of students, the people of New York and others around the world.
- **NOM-82 [Official] Vision.** Cornell aspires to be the exemplary comprehensive research university for the 21st century. Faculty, staff and students thrive at Cornell because of its unparalleled combination of quality and breadth; its open, collaborative and innovative culture; its founding commitment to diversity and inclusion; its vibrant rural and urban campuses; and its land-grant legacy of public engagement.
- **NOM-83 [Official] Public engagement.** The site describes Cornell as the designated land-grant institution for New York state and "the only land-grant university in the Ivy League". Use the full paragraph at https://brand.cornell.edu/messaging rather than a paraphrase.
