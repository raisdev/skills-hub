## 1. Hard rules for code and generated assets

- **LOG-01 [Derived, from LOG-20 to LOG-23]** Never draw, trace, recreate, recolor, crop, stretch, screen or edit a Cornell logo, seal or wordmark, including as SVG or CSS. Do not generate one from text. Insert an official file the user downloaded, or leave a clearly marked placeholder and tell the user where to get the file (section 9).
- **LOG-02 [Official]** The Cornell logo and seal appear only in carnelian, black or white. (https://brand.cornell.edu/design-center/colors/ and /logos/academic)
- **LOG-03 [Official]** Websites hosted outside the cornell.edu domain **should not display a Cornell logo or seal**. All websites on cornell.edu must follow the brand guidelines. (https://brand.cornell.edu/resources/faqs) The wording is "should not", not "must not". So do not add a Cornell logo to a page on github.io or another non-Cornell host, and when one is found report it with that exact wording.
- **LOG-04 [Official]** A vendor's use of the Cornell logo on its own website must be approved by Brand Communications through the Use of Name or Logo form. (FAQ)
- **LOG-05 [Official]** A Cornell logo or seal in an email signature is unnecessary: it inflates the message and can render badly, and the Cornell email address already identifies the sender. (FAQ)

## 2. Which logo or seal to use

Source: the usage chart on https://brand.cornell.edu/logos/ (print/web availability read from the page markup).

| Asset | Size rule stated by the site | Print | Web |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LOG-10** Bold Logo | greater than 3/4" tall | yes | **no** |
| **LOG-11** Bold Seal | greater than 3/4" tall | yes | **no** |
| **LOG-12** Simple Logo | between 73 and 120 pixels | no | yes |
| **LOG-13** Simple Seal | between 73 and 120 pixels | no | yes |
| **LOG-14** Reduced Logo | must be 45 pixels tall | no | yes |
| **LOG-15** Reduced Wordmark | used below 3/4" | yes | yes |

All [Official]. Also: "When using the simple seal, the dimension must not exceed 120 pixels." Bold assets are for print, simple and reduced for web. The logo is the seal plus the "Cornell University" wordmark; the seal and wordmark are the parts of the lockup (anatomy image on the logos page).

- **LOG-16 [Unspecified]** The site does not say whether the 73 to 120 px range for the simple seal applies to width, height or diameter. **[Derived]** The seal is round, so the dimensions are equal; apply the range to both and keep the aspect ratio (LOG-22).

## 3. Placement and treatment rules

- **LOG-20 [Official]** The logo must have enough contrast against its background to be clear and legible, on any background that provides it. Use the bold line logo over an image.
- **LOG-21 [Official]** **Clear space:** text, headlines, photographs or illustrations should never be closer to the logo than **1/4 the diameter of the seal**.
- **LOG-22 [Official]** **No watermarking, screening or cropping of the seal** in any design.
- **LOG-23 [Official]** "Do not attempt to create art for the Cornell logo, seal or wordmark for any application."
- **LOG-24 [Official]** The Cornell logo appears **only once** on a homepage or communications piece.
- **LOG-25 [Official]** Logos must be reproduced at a size that is legible.
- **LOG-26 [Official]** The site offers an outro video and a favicon set as gated downloads (see `_sources.md`). Use those files rather than rebuilding them.
- **LOG-27 [Unspecified]** The site does not say whether each page of a multi-page piece (a slide deck, a PDF, a multi-page site) counts as its own "communications piece" for LOG-24. Default to one logo per deck or document, and ask Brand Communications or follow the official template before repeating it on every slide.
- **LOG-28 [Unspecified]** The site does not say whether the official favicon counts as a logo under LOG-03. Do not add a Cornell favicon to a site outside cornell.edu without asking.

## 4. Academic lockups (colleges and schools)

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/logos/academic

- **LOG-30 [Official]** This lockup configuration is used **exclusively** to represent colleges and schools. Departments and units within a college can be built into the school's lockup if it is part of the academic mission. For programs, centers and institutes, request a consultation.
- **LOG-31 [Official]** Checklist: the hairline and seal are **equal height**; only black, red or white is used for the seal; the lockup is not used for anything other than a college or school; the **space between the first letter and the seal is half the width of the seal**; the seal is **at least 73 px square**; if the seal would be smaller than 73 px, switch to the **45 px logo lockup**.
- **LOG-32 [Official]** Lockups are "responsive in nature": the layout responds to the container's size and does not require one fixed expression. The brand site demonstrates this with SVGs that move the seal and wordmark at 499 px, 500-767 px and smaller widths (`/assets/css/responsive-svg.css`). The site offers no downloadable responsive logo code; do not re-implement it. Ask the college communications lead for the official assets.
- **LOG-33 [Official]** Each college or school has a communications team that supplies lockup assets (FAQ; directory at https://universityrelations.cornell.edu/resources/comms-leads/).

## 5. Non-academic units

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/logos/non-academic

- **LOG-40 [Official]** A non-academic logo, wordmark or other identifier may share primacy with and sit near the seal logo but **may not use a lockup configuration**.
- **LOG-41 [Official]** Affiliates (organizations not part of the university that still use the Cornell name) have their own responsive examples; contact Brand Communications about affiliate criteria.

## 6. Brand architecture (who may have a logo)

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/logos/brand-architecture and /resources/terminology

- **LOG-50 [Official]** Cornell uses a **branded house** model: all units use a unified written, verbal and visual identity aligned to the primary brand. The **house of brands** model, where units have independent identities with no visible tie to Cornell, is **prohibited** for Cornell units.
- **LOG-51 [Official]** Levels: primary brand; primary extensions (colleges and schools, which must lock up to the university seal in primary communications); secondary extensions (research institutes and centers); tertiary extensions (commercialized entities); faculty, staff and student groups; initiatives, campaigns and events.
- **LOG-52 [Official]** Research institutes and centers are advised to use their name together with the university identity and not develop a distinct identity. A distinct identity requires a proposal to Brand Communications **before** any work. University-level institutes must also use the Cornell logo or name wherever the distinct identity appears.
- **LOG-53 [Official]** Commercialized entities may create a distinct identity, after review by Brand Communications, and must show Cornell or the college lockup on merchandise.
- **LOG-54 [Official]** Faculty, staff and student groups may create a distinct identity only after review. **Groups may not use or edit any Cornell trademark** in their identity. **Faculty-led lab groups are not permitted to create or promote their own identity** and should use the university or college identity.
- **LOG-55 [Official]** Initiatives, campaigns and events may create a distinct identity after review and should use it together with the Cornell brand or college lockup on merchandise.
- **LOG-56 [Official]** The FAQ says individual departments, programs, centers and institutes "may not have logos". This conflicts with LOG-52 (see SRC-05). Resolve by sending the request to Brand Communications; do not design one.
- **LOG-57 [Official]** Logos of student, staff and faculty groups are not promoted on central university platforms (homepage, Chronicle).
- **LOG-58 [Derived]** A homemade mark, monogram or tile standing in for a unit logo on a web page (for example a colored square with initials) is not an approved logo type under this architecture. Flag it as a question for Brand Communications, not as a certain violation, because the site does not define "logo" for such marks beyond "symbols presented together with an entity's name".

## 7. Multi-college units

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/logos/multi-college-logo-hierarchy

- **LOG-60 [Official]** A department, program or center, single- or multi-college, follows the visual identity of its **lead college or unit**: same design framework, templates and brand assets as that college's department sites, with the lead unit creating the approved wordmark. The Cornell University logo appears alongside the unit name.
- **LOG-61 [Official]** Where Cornell is positioned as the top-level owner of a center or program, the Cornell University logo appears in the leading position.
- **LOG-62 [Official]** Partner colleges get visibility through storytelling (features, program highlights, faculty) and may be acknowledged in an introductory paragraph below the banner, with links.
- **LOG-63 [Official]** For a formal partner mention in the footer: show the lead partner's logo and list the others as text only, **or** show up to two partner logos or wordmarks. With three or more partners, keep the lead college logo first and list the rest as text.

## 8. Trademark symbols

Source: https://brand.cornell.edu/merchandising and the licensee guide.

- **LOG-70 [Official]** The Cornell logo is a trademark and must carry the trademark symbol ™. The name "Cornell University" is registered, so when the logotype is used alone it must carry ®. Not used where the mark would be illegible. Not required on banners, flags and signs.
- **LOG-71 [Official]** The licensee guide says ™ and ® should **not** be used on licensed products. (SRC-04)
- **LOG-72 [Unspecified]** The site says nothing about ™ or ® on websites. Do not add them to a page unless the user says Brand Communications asked.

## 9. Where to get the artwork

- **LOG-80 [Official]** Official downloads (logos, seals, favicon, outro video, color palettes, presentation templates): https://brand.cornell.edu/resources/downloads. The logo and template downloads require an institutional sign-in. College, school and department lockups come from your communications director.
- **LOG-81 [Official]** Logo use requests and consultations: Use of Name or Logo form https://apps.univcomm.cornell.edu/brand-forms/forms/endorsement.html, Consultation request https://apps.univcomm.cornell.edu/brand-forms/forms/consult.html, Report misuse https://apps.univcomm.cornell.edu/brand-forms/forms/misuse.html, general contact brand@cornell.edu.
- **LOG-82 [Derived]** This plugin bundles no Cornell artwork, on purpose: logo use is governed by Policy 4.10 and this repository is public.
